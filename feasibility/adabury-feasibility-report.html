01

Summary and verdict

The school works as a business once it is established, but it needs about twice the planned start-up money.

In the base case Adabury reaches 780 students by Year 5, makes its first net profit in year 3, and earns about PKR 44 million a year by Year 5 (about PKR 51 million by Year 10). The cash invested is paid back in year 8.

However, losses in the first two years, together with the fit-out, mean the school is up to PKR 20.8 crore down before it turns the corner. With PKR 10 crore invested, about PKR 10.8 crore more is needed. Section 11 shows how to close most of that gap.

Recommendation: go ahead to the site search and registration stage, but sign a lease only once the funding plan in Section 11 is agreed. The worst case (20% fewer students and 5% lower fees) does not make a profit within 10 years, so a strong first intake is essential.

02

The opportunity

Over 13 years of running The Deenway School, the founder has seen a large group of parents who want strong character and an Islamic environment for their children, but who will not choose a school that looks strictly traditional. They want a world-class school that also builds character.

In Faisalabad, these middle and upper-middle income families currently choose between two kinds of school:

  • Premium chains (Beaconhouse, LGS, Roots and others): strong academics and facilities, but values and Islamic learning are not the core promise.
  • Islamic schools: strong on Quran and values, but rarely seen as offering Cambridge-level academics or a modern, premium experience.

Adabury sits between them: Cambridge academics, with character first ("Adab is the first grace"). It is priced below the premium chains and looks and feels like a leading school, with a crest rooted in Faisalabad's Clock Tower.

Target families

  • Business, textile and professional families on and around the Canal Road belt.
  • Parents moving their child after Grade 2 from a pre-school or a general private school, looking for stronger values and stronger academics.
  • Families who want Quran, Arabic and good manners taught seriously, alongside O Levels.

04

Location and building

Area: the Canal Road belt (Canal Road, Gulberg, Kohinoor City, Eden Valley and nearby). This is where the target families live, it is easy to reach, and it signals a premium school. The trade-off is higher rent and more competition, which the model reflects.

What the building must have

  • About 6–8 kanal, with room for 36 classrooms at full size (9 grades × 2 blocks × 2 sections).
  • Two fully separate blocks, girls' and boys', each with its own entrance, prayer hall, washrooms, staff room, library, science lab and computer lab.
  • A safe drop-off area for vans and cars off the main road, which matters on busy Canal Road.
  • Permission for school use from the Faisalabad Development Authority, and room for solar panels on the roof.

Rent

The model assumes PKR 20 lakh a month from April 2028, rising 10% a year, with 6 months' deposit and 4 months' rent during fit-out. Canal Road listings show 1–2 kanal buildings at roughly PKR 3.5–8 lakh, so a two-block school building is scaled up from those. Rent is the single biggest cost to negotiate. A 10-year lease with rent increases capped at 10%, two rent-free months for fit-out, and a first right to buy are the targets.

Academic and Tarbiyah model

Academic pathway: Cambridge International

StageGradesProgramme
Junior3–5Cambridge Primary: English, Maths, Science, Global Perspectives, Computing, plus Urdu and Islamiyat to the Punjab curriculum
Middle6–8Cambridge Lower Secondary, with Cambridge Checkpoint tests in Grade 8
O Level9–11Cambridge O Level: about 8 subjects, including the compulsory Islamiyat, Pakistan Studies and Urdu

The Adabury Tarbiyah programme (the difference parents pay for)

  • Quran every day: Nazra and Tajweed, with selected surahs memorised, plus an optional Hifz track in the afternoon.
  • Arabic from Grade 3, focused on understanding the Quran and daily duas.
  • Seerah and Islamic studies taught through stories and projects.
  • Six character values, rated every term on the report card: Adab, Amanah, Ihsan, Sabr, Shukr and Khidmah. A "Value of the month" is shared with families.
  • Zuhr prayer in congregation in each block. A mentor teacher meets each small group of students every week.
  • Service learning, debating and leadership roles, so students become confident and kind.

A typical day

7:45 assembly and Quran → 8:15–13:15 Cambridge lessons (with a break) → 13:15 Zuhr → 13:35–14:15 Tarbiyah, clubs or Hifz track. Fridays end after Jumu'ah preparation.

Uniform

The approved claret-and-white uniform, with no tie. The school hijab is compulsory for girls. See the uniform page.

06

Governance

Legal form: a separate private limited company registered with SECP, which owns the Adabury brand. It should be fully separate from The Deenway School, as decided. The Adabury name and crest should be registered as trademarks with IPO Pakistan before launch. A tax adviser should confirm whether a company, an association of persons or a Section 42 non-profit structure works best.

Board of Governors (5–7 members)

  • Chair: the founder.
  • Two independent education experts, ideally with Cambridge school leadership experience.
  • One finance and audit expert (a chartered accountant).
  • One Shariah and Tarbiyah scholar, who also chairs the advisory council below.
  • One parent representative, from Year 2.
  • The Principal, attending without a vote.

The Board meets every quarter and has three committees: Academic and Tarbiyah, Finance and Audit, and People and Safeguarding. An advisory Shariah and Tarbiyah Council of two or three scholars approves the Tarbiyah curriculum and policies once a year.

Salary benchmarks: Lahore O Level teacher posts advertised at PKR 50,000–70,000 for early-career teachers (Taraki listing); Punjab minimum wage PKR 40,000 from July 2025 (ProPakistani).

11

Closing the funding gap

The base case needs about PKR 20.8 crore at its lowest point (Year 2) against PKR 10 crore budgeted. These measures can be combined. The savings shown are approximate and should be tested in the model before a lease is signed.

1. Islamic bank lease (Ijarah) for equipment

Lease the air conditioning, solar system, IT and smart boards (about PKR 4 crore) instead of buying them. Monthly rentals replace a large upfront payment.

Reduces start-up cash by about PKR 3–4 crore

2. Landlord-funded fit-out

Choose a landlord who builds the partitions, washrooms and prayer halls to the school's design, in return for a somewhat higher rent over a 10-year lease.

Reduces start-up cash by about PKR 2–3 crore

3. Softer rent in the first two years

Negotiate a stepped rent: for example 60% in Year 1 and 80% in Year 2, recovered later in the lease.

Reduces early losses by about PKR 1–1.5 crore

4. Strong first intake

Target 350 or more students in Year 1 through early admissions from September 2027, founding-family discounts and open days.

Every 50 extra students: about PKR 1.5–2 crore better

5. A partner or a bank facility for the rest

A minority investor, or a running-finance line from an Islamic bank secured on fee income, covering Years 1–2 only.

Covers whatever remains, likely PKR 1–3 crore

6. Fallback: a smaller first phase

Open Grades 3–8 only in Year 1 and add O Level from Year 2. This means fewer labs and teachers at the start, but delays O Level income.

Reduces Year 1 costs; test in the model

With measures 1–3 together, the gap shrinks to roughly PKR 3–5 crore, which brings the total close to the PKR 12 crore upper end of the founder's range. Measure 5 can then cover the rest.

Licences and approvals

These need to be confirmed with a local consultant, because requirements change.

  • Company registration with SECP, and NTN and tax registration with FBR.
  • Trademark registration of the name and crest with IPO Pakistan.
  • Building approval for school use from the Faisalabad Development Authority.
  • Registration of the private school with the Punjab school education authorities (District Education Authority, Faisalabad).
  • Approval as a Cambridge International school (Cambridge Assessment International Education), which can take several months.
  • Fire safety, building safety and health certificates. Staff registered with EOBI and Punjab social security.
