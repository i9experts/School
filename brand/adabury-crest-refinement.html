The same idea, drawn better. The star now sits on a gold band at the top of the shield, the way the top band of a heraldic shield is used for the most important symbol. From above, central Faisalabad is a small square (the Clock Tower) with eight bazaars running out from it, joined by the round Gol Bazaar. Here that city plan is drawn as the eight-point Islamic star. Each road ends in one of the star's points.

Strength: one symbol carries three meanings: faith (the khatam star), the city (eight bazaars), and the school (eight core values, one per point, which teachers can use in class). It belongs to no other school and makes a great story for parents and the press: "Eight bazaars, one star." Weakness: the city link needs one sentence of explanation the first time.

Making Adabury known in Faisalabad

Beyond the logo

"Adab is the first grace."
Urdu tagline for hoardings, admissions and the gate
1. Bilingual signage. English name with the Urdu name in Nastaliq script on the gate, vans and hoardings. Parents and grandparents recognise it at a glance, and it feels local and premium at the same time.
2. An Urdu tagline everyone already knows. The well-known line "Adab pehla qareena hai mohabbat ke qareenon mein" starts with the school's own name. Using its first half as a tagline makes the brand feel familiar from day one.
3. One story to repeat: "Eight bazaars, one star." If you choose option 3, launch with a short video that flies over the Clock Tower and shows the eight bazaars turning into the Adabury star.
4. A signature colour on the streets. Claret school vans, a claret blazer and gold-lettered gates. A colour no other Faisalabad school owns quickly becomes "the Adabury colour".

Your decision

  1. Pick one crest: 1, 2, 3 or 4, or a mix, e.g. "option 3 with the olive sprig from option 1".
  2. Motto: keep Adab · Ilm · Ihsan, or use another, e.g. Character · Knowledge · Excellence.
  3. Urdu: approve the Urdu spelling ادبری اسکول and the tagline ادب پہلا قرینہ ہے, or suggest your own.
