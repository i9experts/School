A claret shield with an eight-point star (faith and Islamic heritage), a chevron (a roof: the school as a home and shelter) and an olive sprig (the blessed tree of Surah An-Nur, and growth).

Best for: the strongest "established British school" look. Claret and gold are traditional colours of English public schools and look rich on blazers, ties and gates.

The letter A is drawn as an Islamic pointed arch, with a small star inside it. The A sits in a round seal with the school's name, city and founding year, like the seal of an old university.

Best for: certificates, report cards, the school stamp and the main gate. The arch-A works alone as a clean modern icon, so one system covers both heritage and digital use.

A traditional fanoos lantern with an arch-shaped window and a flame inside: the light of knowledge, carried with good manners. The lantern recalls Ramadan evenings and old Lahore and Cairo streets without using any religious text.

Best for: a warm, memorable symbol that no other Faisalabad school uses. Lights up well at night as gate signage and suits annual events ("Lantern Day" for the graduating class).

An elegant italic wordmark, like a handwritten signature, underlined with a single stroke that ends in an eight-point star. The star alone is the icon.

Best for: a boutique, premium feel, like a luxury brand. It's the most minimal option and relies on beautiful execution. It is the least "school crest", so some parents may read it as less traditional.
